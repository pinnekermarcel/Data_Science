\section*{2. Your observation, your question, your target number}
\textbf{Observation:} Within the French bicycle segment, order values exhibit strong right-skewness driven by high-volume reseller bulk purchases alongside regular individual online orders. \\
\textbf{Question:} How strongly do B2B wholesale bulk orders distort average transaction metrics compared to typical unit pricing in the France/Bikes slice, and how should anomalous zero-price entries be handled without corrupting historical pricing integrity? \\
\textbf{Target Number:} \textbf{2,071.42~EUR} (raw median of \texttt{LineTotal} across all 2,618 order lines, chosen over the mean of 2,335.46~EUR due to heavy upper-tail skewness).

\section*{6. The decision that matters most}
The most critical data cleaning decision in our slice concerns \textbf{P1}: the handling of the 3 order lines in \texttt{Sales\_SalesOrderDetail} where \texttt{UnitPrice} equals \texttt{0.00~EUR}. We evaluated two competing strategies before finalizing our pipeline.

First, we tested the rejected alternative, \emph{fix when reading}, by imputing the missing prices with the standard \texttt{ListPrice} from \texttt{Production\_Product}. Under this approach, the row count remains unchanged at 2,618 lines, and the raw median \texttt{LineTotal} remains at 2,071.42~EUR (as 3 rows out of 2,618 do not shift the 50th percentile), while total slice revenue increases artificially by the sum of the three catalog list prices. However, we \dec{rejected} automatic imputation because overwriting transaction prices destroys historical fidelity. In an enterprise ERP system like AdventureWorks, a zero unit price can represent an intentional promotional giveaway, warranty replacement, or special B2B volume rebate. Fabricating revenue where none was billed introduces silent bias into financial reporting.

Second, we executed our chosen action, \dec{quarantine}, isolating all rows with \texttt{UnitPrice == 0} into a separate \texttt{reject\_table}. This reduces the active analytical dataset from 2,618 to 2,615 rows (-0.11\%), while keeping the target median \texttt{LineTotal} stable at 2,071.42~EUR. We chose quarantine because strict separation of anomalous records protects downstream pricing models from zero-price distortion while preserving full auditability for business owners to inspect the quarantined rows later.

\section*{7. Metadata and personal data}
Full dataset documentation, the 10-column data dictionary, known issues, and FAIR scores are stored in \texttt{awc\_flat.metadata.yaml}.

\textbf{Personal Data \& Identifiability:} While the flat extract omits direct names and addresses, it still contains indirect personal data under GDPR principles. Specifically, combining \texttt{CustomerID} with \texttt{OrderDate}, exact \texttt{LineTotal} amounts, and \texttt{TerritoryName} acts as a quasi-identifier capable of re-identifying individual B2C buyers when linked to payment or shipping logs. To enforce $k$-anonymity before sharing, we would first \dec{coarsen} \texttt{OrderDate} from exact days to year-month granularity and pseudonymize or bin \texttt{CustomerID} into customer segments.
